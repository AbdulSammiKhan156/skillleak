A course outcome is a promise.
An assessment is evidence.

SkillLeak AI checks whether a course's exams actually test what its learning outcomes promise, and whether the course covers the skills employers ask for. Every finding comes with its evidence, and faculty make the final call.

The problem

Engineering programmes in Pakistan run Outcome-Based Education. Every Course Learning Outcome (CLO) has a cognitive level, C1 Remember to C6 Create, and course files must show that assessments measure it. That cross-check is done by hand, so mismatches slip through.

Promises vs evidence

A CLO can promise "diagnose faults" (C4) while the only question tagged to it asks students to list steps (C1).

Numbers that mislead

Such a CLO can still be reported as attained, because everyone passes a recall question.

Employers notice

3 of the 4 public Pakistani network-engineering postings we sourced explicitly ask candidates to troubleshoot.

How it works

The AI does the reading. Code does the checking. Faculty make the decision.

FacultyUploadCourse outline with CLOs, lab manual, exam papers, marks sheet, job postings.
AIExtractEvery CLO, its level and PLO, and every exam question with its marks.
AI × 2RateWhat each question makes a student do (C1-C6): once with the CLO, once blind.
CodeVerify & decideQuotes must exist in the source. Fixed rules give each CLO a finding.
FacultyActCorrect any rating, generate an aligned question or lab, export the course-file pack.

100%

of the 40 synthetic students reach the attainment threshold for CLO-5 on the demo marks sheet.

Potential attainment-validity concern

Attained under the configured thresholds, but the supplied evidence reaches only C1-C2 while the CLO states C4. It's a prompt to check whether the CLO is assessed elsewhere, not proof.

Suggested fix

An AI-drafted lab-exam task asks students to diagnose injected faults in a Packet Tracer topology, with a marking guide. Faculty approve or edit it.

Why you can trust the output

LLMs misjudge cognitive level, often by one level. SkillLeak is designed around that.

Rules, not vibes

  • Potential mismatch only if both AI ratings are below the CLO level
  • Needs review if the ratings straddle the level, the level is only inferred, or the aligned-mark share is under 50%
  • Every AI quote is checked against the source; invented ones are rejected
aligned-mark share = marks at or above CLO level ÷ marks linked to the CLO

Measured, with caveats

62.7% exact accuracy for a keyword baseline we measured on a public 600-question labelled set (73.8% within one level). The labels' annotation provenance is undocumented.

A separate published study reports zero-shot LLMs at about 72-73% on a similar set (arXiv:2511.10903). That's why SkillLeak flags disagreement and keeps faculty in charge.

Honest limits & disclosure

  • Decision support, not a PEC/HEC accreditation verdict.
  • AI ratings can be wrong: disagreements are flagged, reasons are shown, and faculty can override.
  • The demo course and marks are synthetic and labelled. The 4 job postings are real public listings (closed) with source links; that's a small sample, not national demand.
  • Built during AICON'26 (9-10 Oct 2026) with Claude as an AI coding assistant. Runtime AI: the LLM API selected in the app. Open-source libraries: Streamlit, PyMuPDF, Plotly, pandas.
